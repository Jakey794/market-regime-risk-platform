# Market Regime and Portfolio Risk Platform — Final Research Report

## Abstract

This report documents a leakage-aware portfolio risk and regime-analysis platform spanning data ingestion, feature engineering, interpretable and probabilistic regime models, stress testing, and no-look-ahead backtesting. The platform is designed for risk research, not return prediction.

## Research question

How can portfolio risk, market regimes, stress exposures, and simple risk-aware allocation rules be measured in a reproducible, look-ahead-resistant research platform?

## Dataset and ETF proxies

The research universe uses liquid ETF proxies (for example SPY, QQQ, XIU.TO, EFA, EEM, and sector ETFs such as XLK) configured in `configs/default_universe.yaml` and `configs/sample_portfolio.yaml`. These are market proxies, not a complete institutional security master.

## Data quality

Data quality checks cover missing values, duplicate dates, alignment, and freshness. Free ETF history may be shorter than requested stress windows; coverage warnings are required in those cases.

## Feature engineering

Week 5 regime features use trailing volatility, correlation, drawdown, and momentum signals. Scalers are fit on the training period only.

## Risk metrics

The risk engine provides returns, volatility, drawdown, Sharpe/Sortino/Calmar, VaR/CVaR, beta, correlation, concentration, and risk contribution.

## Model methodology

Regime models include an interpretable threshold baseline, KMeans, GMM, GaussianHMM, and ruptures change-point detectors. Models fit on training data only; economic labels are derived from training-period state statistics.

## Backtest methodology

Backtests distinguish signal, decision, execution, and return realization dates; signals are shifted to prevent same-close execution; transaction costs and turnover are accounted for; future mutations must not alter earlier positions.

## Failure analysis

1) Regime-label instability across seeds/specs; economic relabeling helps but does not create unique true states. 2) State-identification ambiguity without a unique causal map. 3) Window sensitivity of trailing features (21d/63d/252d). 4) Number-of-state sensitivity in interpretability and duration stats. 5) Non-stationarity of risk regimes after the training window. 6) ETF proxy limitations for sector/region/factor exposures. 7) Free-data history gaps and revisions. 8) Crisis correlation breakdown versus calm-sample diversification. 9) VaR/CVaR sample dependence and unseen-tail limits. 10) Simplified proportional transaction costs. 11) Bull-market underperformance of defensive/de-risking rules. 12) Backtest overfitting and model-selection bias if rules are chosen after seeing results.

## Limitations

- Not a stock-prediction system.
- Not financial advice.
- ETF data quality and history constraints.
- Model metrics are not universally comparable across families.
- Transaction-cost model is proportional and simplified.
